# Hard-water marking-scheme correction

A live Integrated Science practical subpart asked for a health effect of drinking very hard water and marked “kidney stones” or “scaling in the kidneys” as a general correct answer. This is not a suitable unqualified school-science marking rule.

The Drinking Water Inspectorate describes scaling in appliances and reduced soap lather, and notes potential dietary benefits of calcium and magnesium. It does not establish the claimed “kidney scaling” effect. A large prospective cohort found no significant overall association between water hardness and kidney stones, while reporting more nuanced subgroup and mineral-specific associations. Therefore, a simple one-mark general causal claim is misleading; use established domestic effects or accurately qualified evidence instead. [1] [2]

The generator should reject such unsupported general renal-harm answers and rewrite the subpart within the same curriculum and mark allocation. This note concerns exam-content accuracy, not individual medical advice.
